The easiest way to make a page feel alive is to make something move.

The harder question is what should be allowed to stay still.

A sketchbook can turn a page. A photograph can drift into view. A line of text usually needs something simpler: time, space, and a reason to keep reading.

## Three kinds of movement

**Signature motion** carries the atmosphere. The sketchbook page turn belongs here.

**Quiet motion** helps a reader feel where they are: a menu opening, a link changing, a small transition between sections.

**No motion** is also a choice. A paragraph should be able to sit still long enough to be read twice.

## Let stillness work too

Reduced-motion preferences matter because the writing should survive without the choreography. The page turn can become a simple page switch, tilt can disappear, and decorative transitions can shorten or stop.

The atmosphere may change, but the words remain exactly where they need to be.
